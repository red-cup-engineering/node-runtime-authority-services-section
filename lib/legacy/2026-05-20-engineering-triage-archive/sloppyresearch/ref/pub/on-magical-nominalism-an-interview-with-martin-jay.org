  - Jay highlights he began with "all of the typcial modern prejudices against revivals of [[id:e26cf5e7-f8c0-4689-b252-cb1068aa817e][belief]] in [[id:cb4c83e4-1c13-42c9-bcc8-126ff5899f1a][supernatural]] or [[id:b8928dc3-0b66-4fe9-8b2a-45409371a3ba][occult practices]], and still largely retain them.-

- Prosser asks about nominalism: why now, and why magical.
  - Jay got especially fascinated with the [[id:82f52e10-e98c-42c7-b5dc-949ee13f9973][history of nominalism]] when he realized it wasn't just an esoteric philosophical debate within realism, but central to larger, often very heated, disccussions about "the most fundamental and abiding of human concerns."
    - Our understanding of [[id:8b531a9d-45d6-476b-a05b-3914eb520a4f][God]]
    - our [[id:40a4244e-a940-40f0-a4bb-0e6e265e96f3][relationship]] to Him
    - our belief in the human ability to know, make sense of, and explain the workings of His creation.
    - our capacity to have aesthetic experiences with privileged instances of that creation
    - our hopes for improving an imperfect world and creating modes of living together that nuture human flourishing
  - Some concerns are theological, others philosophical, others are social or cultural
  - All are affected by nominalist critique of real universals
  - Blumenberg argues one big critique was the "upsurge of human self-assertion" that came in when "belief in a harmonious cosmos ruled by divinely mandated reason" faded.
    - In Horkheimer/Adorno's terms, this set up the hegemony of humans over the real, most practically domination of nature.
  - Now we're seeing the costs of that human-self assertion
  - Why now?
    - Living at the dawn of the [[id:ece9a843-d90d-4673-89e5-6778535fc737][Anthropocene]] (if that's where we are) makes it imperative to reflect on the limits of the self-assertion abetted by the nominalist revolution.
      - */emsenn/*: That feels like a statement I agree with.
  - Jay says other attempts to restore faith in the real universal haven't convinced him:
    - [[id:b899c18e-49e1-4070-be23-58854853c105][natural law theory]] or [[id:665040cc-4b1f-40fb-bc6e-c2036803f2e0][Neo-Scholasticism]]
    - "do not succeed... in relieving the anxieties unleashed by the... ideological comfort... real universals once provided."
      - */emsenn/*: This surely ties into [[id:4360a3e7-109a-4838-8483-7adc34ddf316][neurotic intellectual]] stuff.
  - Jay says nominalism can't be easily refuted
    - suggests there may be variants that are "less subject-centered, less intent on subssuming particulars under concepts, less complicit with the domination of nature"
      - */emsenn/*: does my relational process theory meet the criteria of a maginal nominalism?
    - that nominalism would be magical because it would "recover some of the awe-inspiring [[id:4826d09d-607a-49f9-beb5-7f3ee507340f][mystery]] of our contact with the world prior to its being reduced... to a 'standing reserve' for technological purposes."
      - Okay, that sounds a lot like [[id:e51a8834-6b11-4b10-9120-5398b50b6136][Lakotaean mystery]], at least when explained to a nominalist perspective.
- Prosser highlights the book's scope and outlines its movements, asking why the process described is important:
  - Jay presents nominalism (as [[id:43702296-4fa0-4094-8470-a777bf79372c][via moderna]]) displacing [[id:ad164bf9-ecad-43e6-9f5f-37ae6f6fda9a][Scholastic realism]] ([[id:f53e512e-1b4c-42e5-833f-54287808d4ea][via antiqua]]), foreshadowing [[id:344442a8-aa6a-4e6f-83fb-df623bb35b96][modern thinking]] and perhaps [[id:9091b8ea-b1df-4f8e-90f6-1c721bd2110d][modernity]]
  - Jay highlights history of nominalism:
    - originating in theological disputes in the Middle Ages
    - occuring from stress betewen Hebraic and Greek components of Christian theology
      - As [[id:5a6c6764-e9e1-4f1c-a40f-d1acfa38ab04][Tertullian]] put it, [[id:6af7e52f-eab3-47a5-9224-81bb4dc2b1fc][Jerusalem]] over [[id:fc575e55-5f61-4139-932f-bd76871d000d][Athens]]
	- God's will over the cosmic order previously ordained.
    - Ockham was a [[id:90181a97-a0b6-4192-b2af-fdf3c43ceee2][Franciscan]]
      - instrumental in overthrowing [[id:27c2d619-41af-4b77-9ac1-5df5646f14ef][Aristotelian theology]] associated with [[id:302a3319-6d4b-4bf2-b509-8c0fca5db619][Dominicanism]], i.e. [[id:7fd79463-840a-4004-a485-cc2aadae2af4][Thomas Aquinas]] and [[id:67589c2c-91e1-48ff-8f36-d850eb341cff][Scholasticism]].
    - Privileging will over reason (according to Blumenberg, according to Jay) became the defining characteristic of human self-assertion
      - That term, human self-assertion, keeps coming up. I am very curious what it's formal meaning is. It seems somewhere between [[id:2f55705d-46ef-429e-87e4-91dd9536bf14][individualism]] and [[id:2a1d9910-0b7c-4f93-a26b-6b4c3a531cce][anthropocentricism]].
      - conventional nominalism is "subjective imposing of conceptual order of bestowing of arbitrary names on a world of radical contingency"
	- this is where [[id:b75116e2-1f59-4313-893c-9455251ba7be][kyriontoeidonic theory]] might be relevant
	- math as nominalist, when applied to the world.
      - magical nominalism
	- rather than displace divine omnipotence/God's will onto humans
	  - celebrate resistance to "subjective imposition in unique singularities that escape subsumption under the general rules of either [[id:718e022e-aa16-4412-a7be-78b6d27f0c0e][rationalist realism]] or [[id:b2bd7984-fb59-4e9a-883a-3d6323dd34d1][conventional nominalism]].
	- magincal nominalism maps to [[id:e7814e3d-f5a4-40c8-ba77-85752ca1ced5][mystical nominalism]] ([[id:2082ad4b-f2ca-48d2-99a9-7b97fa7f289d][Ernst Bloch]]) and Jewish nominalism (Bielik-Robson), but Jay prefers magical:
	  - challenges the foundational premise of political theology:
	    - that religious idiom is substantively prior and thus superior
	  - Jay wants to show that religion itself ([[id:bd9ecf3c-2174-4918-bff2-26d1700364b5][Jewish]], [[id:0d10611a-01bc-4e65-916a-fd8901486816][Christian]], or [[id:8b9161ac-be68-4282-836e-f1cf140a9b16][other]]) "derives at least some of its original impulses from earlier human relations to the world, in which certain singularities-things, events, words-possess whatever causes a sense of wonder and awe in those who encounter thing."
	    - religious and theological intermediates "preserve" a "transfigured form"
	    -"when they are recovered in our own age, sparks of their earlier magical origins remain available for reignition."
- Prosser highlights that Jay situates [[id:9bb6c2a7-bb47-4bc0-aa89-cee60297edca][critical theory]] within a larger [[id:f7265b7a-7e19-4f86-b8d2-d2f96eb9f19e][philosophical arc]].
  - Prosser points to [[id:f8eff3ce-62ab-4d6d-af0e-31d7f38cc50f][Reason after Its Eclipse]] ([[id:e1e38af3-bbde-47d5-9c89-6ed7d3a25ed8][2016ce]]) doing it in relation to [[id:ef0b3c4b-006f-4078-90a0-210f2bea2c0c][Immanuel Kant]] and [[id:12c80e5f-efcf-47cc-b846-ae7e329e7e1c][Hegel]], but /Magical Nominalism/ looks past [[id:61f7f4a0-8e58-4f1a-9114-30e296301176][Marxism]].
  - Jay highlights that every time he returns to the [[id:6480a1be-201c-438e-b24f-a8a266bb5a5e][history of critical theory]], he finds new ways of [[id:d417d6fa-dbb2-43e9-b8c8-59ac4b6664b9][framing]] its [[id:e5987b3f-f07f-47a5-ae7e-d16736b5a04d][narrative]], due to what Jay says Prosser was right to call different [[id:e773a584-c62a-4bd3-b399-928f07541608][throughlines]] (so I guess I'll make that a term).
  - Way back in [[id:1990834c-af21-4daa-9694-7fd2b72199e2][1984ce]], in [[id:28f3f274-e0ec-4b62-bdf7-28520ae273d2][Marxism and Totality]], Jay situated critical thought within the [[id:618bfc01-dd58-495d-adb3-2eeab8d34116]["Western Marxist]] struggle to defend a viable concept of [[id:ce43d2db-88a9-42d7-9d93-6d9bc3000eb2][totality]], which concluded with [[id:44ef1f73-71ca-4a0c-89c7-75643116f115][[Jürgen] Habermas]]'s decentered, non-expressive view of the social whole."
  - /Reason after Its Eclipse/ began with Kant and Hegel, passed through Marx and [[id:3f941867-7570-4ee2-9d07-7be9e07666fb][Georg Lukács]], continued to Horkheimer's "defense of 'objective' reason against its 'instrumental debasement,'" and finished with Habermas's [[id:ce387c77-69f4-479d-ba73-5ad2902f6410][communicative rationality]].
  - In /Magical Nominalism/
    - No Habermas
      - Horkheimer and [[id:651de855-af49-4388-9cd4-8b5ce7f445a0][Marcuse]] are cameos
      - [[id:c979ca4d-1cfa-43bf-b079-f9c416f58ddd][Benjamin]], [[id:21782d8e-0139-4edb-b5b7-53de0e010b4a][Adorno]], and [[id:d5adf2c7-0b5c-4dba-b239-49fcc992c16d][Siegfried Kracauer]] represent critical theory and the Frankfurt School
      - But a lot "protagonists of the story" come from Jay's work on [[id:939857e2-a242-49a8-b60b-d8f2b3283013][20th-century French interrogation of visuality]], esp in the [[id:05d5568a-8b21-4fdf-9afd-8ddcf69b6afa][post-structuralist]] chapters of the book.
  - Jay says magical nominalism can help make sense of an issue in critical theory:
    - dual nature of [[id:e29f715f-dbfd-4895-91eb-d614db25b89d][concreteness]] and [[id:dccd106a-5450-4e41-863a-d02b17fd9468][abstraction]]
      - concreteness is [[id:467e36a5-54fd-4fe8-ae04-5cf31c281ed2][epistemological]] and [[id:98437ac1-8eb1-4c16-bd55-3fce68a6d109][normative]] alternative to abstraction
      - abstraction is often identified with:
	- [[id:0eaf1560-d8a5-46eb-b7ed-0548a915b255][real abstractions]] ([[id:46c8946d-11f6-441b-ad53-edf2553841f2][Alfred Sohn-Rethel]]) ([[id:d8d3b48f-023d-41e4-aa39-bbad3dcd2deb][hegemony]] of exchange relations in [[id:8b838cf9-b626-4609-8a98-332d8236d17e][capitalism]])
	- [[id:660a29cd-2537-4966-8b1c-396707f08f2e][conceptual abstraction]] ([[id:b549a6d3-44ce-4bbc-ae93-b4dd08d75ba8][identitarian thought]])
    - Jay says that [[id:f39587fb-0bae-49df-9606-6bff4687c7c3][dialectical reason]] understood concreteness in a specific way
      - Jay's lineage of [[id:e82aa95e-457c-4e4f-89ff-7406fcf3bdcb][dialectic theory]]:
	- [[id:12c80e5f-efcf-47cc-b846-ae7e329e7e1c][Hegel]]
	- [[id:61f7f4a0-8e58-4f1a-9114-30e296301176][Marxism]]
	- Lukacs
	- [[id:5172ad11-b804-47ac-ae5f-0aa99b629b18][Frankfurt School]]
	- [[id:651de855-af49-4388-9cd4-8b5ce7f445a0][Herbert Marcuse]]'s [[id:8add66e6-349c-45db-bf56-8ef0172b5f28][Reason and Revolution]] is its "highpoint"
      - [[id:b7ce3efa-bbd7-404e-b5b8-47697cee9dea][dialectic concreteness]]: "a '[[id:63521327-43c5-4660-8a88-8c7619a77991][moment]]' in a complexly mediated, dynamically developing whole"
      - [[id:82503ea6-4e11-4317-bb91-a2c25e8d992f][dialectic abstraction]]: a [[id:e5248432-6d1e-436e-9bdc-50fe0fcd3fe6][thing]] extracted from its contexts.
	- classic example of abstraction in [[id:78bf7005-a800-4659-b7c4-f3dca68a0dc3][Hegelian Marxism]]:
	  - [[id:81d0d670-65c0-4b09-b9cd-c002fc4b9308][fetishism of commodities]]
	    - [[id:3ecf31e2-67b6-46c4-923d-2e412aa7d908][commodity]]:
	      - [[id:37f44540-db4d-4b94-ad46-09c4ebf1eeeb][fungible thing]]
	      - [[id:9adb7d35-07dd-477c-8efa-1e169b968d16][origin]] in [[id:f09d5f1e-1e92-4111-8fc2-833b553bae8a][capitalist labor process]] is [[id:64e0c8c4-2c89-4d02-80a6-0f1f58884a48][forgotten]] or [[id:97b1beec-04fc-4f79-9541-1f66af8ff176][repressed]].
    - Jay says adherents of dialectic reason, and the practices it informed, would overcome that [[id:07eafe19-9f39-4d27-a485-675534d88cfa][fetishization]] with [[id:518bb07b-ae7e-4a3c-8c3c-e895f3679dfc][negation]]: undermine the [[id:1fb8bd2d-13bf-4630-8269-451a632c419e][erasing]] of origin and thus false "solidity"
      - "Ultimately, at least for Hegel and Hegelian Marxism, that negativity would be sublated in a concrete totality that would overcome abstraction and end the alienation experienced in the labor process and elsewhere in ccapitalism."
  - Jay says this version of concreteness was an implicit premise of Frankfurt school, but there was another impulse he sensed early on
    - before [[id:25811244-19b4-4832-b88c-c1db96164874][The Dialectical Imagination]] ([[id:b6dacaae-629a-40e6-93d5-76f70a6f4ae7][1972ce]])
    - Jay noticed a "reluctance to adopt the rhetoric of alienation and the restitution of holistic unity, which they attacked as inherently idealist rather than materialist"
      - Genuine materialism entailed the resistance of objects to their subjecetive constitution.
	- Implication:
	- always an excess:
	  - a surplus beyond the reconciled, integrated whole
	  - can never be fully recuperated
      - Jay understood one meaning of Adorno's negative dialectics was resisting the incorporation of different things into a totality.
      - Jay learned Walter Benjamin inspired this version of concreteness, which valorizes unassimilable singularity.
	- Benjamin's distrust of Hegelian dialectics meant he didn't prioritize the cconcept or accept history "as a progressive negation of negations culminating in a grand synthesis of the identical and non-identical."
      - Adorno was more like Hegel than Benjamin, but valued "non-conceptuality"
	- grounded in nominalist critiques of real universals.
	- Jay uses Adorno's attitude toward nominalism in music to highlight Adorno's criticism of conventional nominalism, while "tacitly embracing" magical nominalism.
