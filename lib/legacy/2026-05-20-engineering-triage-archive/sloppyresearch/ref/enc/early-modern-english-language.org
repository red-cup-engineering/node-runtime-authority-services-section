#+index: language!Early Modern English

*/Early Modern English language/* was a [[id:e8bfbd06-21f6-4e9a-a42b-27a93d5c7b21][language]] used in the [[id:05dfb1a8-cecf-48d0-9e82-b1d6bdaf5d8e][British Isles]] from approximately [[id:cecfa265-9b1f-442a-aded-e0bc150ec25c][1500ce]] to the [[id:97ff1b5e-c23d-4d44-943a-748e532dcaf5][late 1600s]]. It followed [[id:e428f9ed-93a6-4f63-ad8c-4834a8664a4c][Middle English]] and came before [[id:7ffc95f8-7815-4b5a-a81a-319d48ac47ac][Modern English]].

*Early Modern English* maintained the [[id:df501f19-6e6e-4498-9054-8bcd6a9d669a][West Germanic grammar]] of /Middle English/ while [[id:d2dd4008-f6be-48cc-a53e-cd64854f2afc][including]] extensive [[id:59cc2103-ae6b-4bc5-b35e-33377eb58174][Latin]] and [[id:80ad4345-3276-4d1d-9d02-cbcd794c7815][French]] vocabulary, due to the [[id:fdb514e9-308b-49bf-b065-d522201b4f8c][Renaissance]] cementing earlier [[id:9b45d6bf-d63b-4dca-b4e1-88f93ad3b480][Norman]] influences. Spoken *Early Modern English* was shaped heavily by the [[id:33bb59b0-93f6-49fd-916b-79c23eeb7bb8][Great Vowel Shift]], while its written form was heavily shaped by the rise of [[id:496fc82b-ce71-45f9-a7c6-29634fdc6e84][English nationalism]] and [[id:8a40acfa-0652-4ec7-b03d-97878b58e445][William Caxton]]'s efforts to [[id:99e714e9-d17f-4bf6-8eef-2929f8cacd6d][standardize]] the language through printing [[id:80ecba92-ca84-4b32-99ae-44d446b24350][texts]] like [[id:a9628043-2129-4eb6-80b1-6b5a67d56f5e][The Canterbury Tales]].

The most famous works of Early Modern English are the works of [[id:4c768b5a-be58-482a-ba0f-ee701996c3e7][William Shakespeare]], the [[id:53b7fda3-7c0a-4b62-87d8-a08a2a5ca3e8][King James Bible]], and the texts of [[id:00670d00-60ee-47ce-8113-b5faa4f0b3d0][William Cecil]], [[id:51d0d338-4a45-430d-9d4f-4a8c1134802f][King James I]], and [[id:68007899-7cee-47b0-8a47-0de0889ee2b1][Francis Bacon]].
