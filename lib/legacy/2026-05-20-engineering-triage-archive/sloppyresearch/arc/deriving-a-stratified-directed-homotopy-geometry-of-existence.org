* Deriving a Stratified Directed Homotopy Geometry of Existence

To claim that any object exists is not trivial.

To make the claim, it must necessarily be claimed that the object occupies some definite position within a structured environment of possible positions.

That is, the claim presupposes not only the object itself, but a surrounding framework in which the object can be distinguished from what it is not, and in which this distinction can be traced through the ways that the object relates to other possible objects.

Let's describe this structured environment as a *stratified directed system*: a setting that organizes phenomena into *strata* of different dimensional orders, linked by *directed paths* that record how one stratum leads into another.

The total collection of such stratified systems, together with the *morphisms* that preserve their directed structure, forms what we'll call the *category of existence*.

Within this category, every individual object is represented by a stratified configuration whose internal organization can be examined through its own network of strata and directed relations.

To assert that an object exists, then, is to assert that there is at least one such configuration that meets such criteria.

Let's call this configuration a *relational cell*: a connected sub-stratified object within the category of existence whose internal *exit-category* (the diagram of its strata and the directed paths between them) specifies the local structure of its being.

In the relational cell, its difference from other things, and the conditions under which it is identified as itself, are represented formally as distinct strata linked by directed relations.

Each relational cell is characterized by three necessary 0-strata *vertex*:

- boundary :: marking the local act of differentiation
- complement :: marking what lies outside the relation
- quotient :: marking identification or sameness

Between every pair of vertices lies a directed *incidence*: a 1-stratum *edge* that records adjacency within the exit-category of the cell.

Each incidence admits *exit-paths* that ascend to a single terminal 2-stratum *face* , the relational cell. The face functions as the unique locus of coherence for the relational cell: every exit-path from a lower stratum terminates there, and the categorical structure of the cell is determined by this convergence.

A *constructible cosheaf* assigns to each stratum a *condition* representing data stable on that stratum. Morphisms in the exit-category induce *pushforwards* along exit-paths, propagating local conditions toward higher strata.  The *colimit* of these assignments defines the global condition of the relational cell.  This colimit resides at the terminal stratum, establishing the cell’s coherence.

Thus, to assert that an object exists is to assert the presence of at least one such relational cell in the category of existence: a sub-stratified object with a unique terminal stratum whose exit-category and cosheaf together realize coherence between differentiation, complementarity, and identification.

** Representing SDHG Objects in Words

Any relational cell in a Stratified Directed Homotopy Geometry (SDHG) category may be represented by the stratified n-simplex formed by the cones of 2-simplex cells that share the face of the cell as their first vertex, the exit path of a cell as their second vertex, and the face of a cell as their third vertex.

As the first vertex is shared, this cell may be expressed as a 2-simplicial complex, ~(Sparky (derives . dog))~, where derives means "a path from one coherence to another," and we know from the above that dog is a cell. We can specify ~(dog (category . animal))~, and so on.

By initially restricting vocabulary to the following vocabulary, which represents proven operators in SDHG, and constructing representations from here, mathematically formal shapes of any concept expressible semantically may be established.
