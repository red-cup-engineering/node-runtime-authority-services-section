** Emergence of Deviation in Collapse Remainder

## Definitions

R is the remainder: the set of values stable under collapse,  
R = { x | κ(x) = x }.  
κ is collapse: the operator defining R.  
δ is a deviation: a value in R that is distinct from at least one other member of R.

## Theorem

A deviation δ exists in R if and only if:

1. κ(δ) = δ — it is collapse-stable  
2. There exists some x ∈ R such that δ ≠ x — it is distinguishable

This defines deviation:  
an element in R that introduces minimal, κ-stable internal difference.

## Formal Expression

δ ∈ R ⇔ κ(δ) = δ ∧ ∃ x ∈ R: δ ≠ x

## Consequence

Difference does not require external structure.  
Deviation is possible within R without disrupting collapse.  
This introduces the first potential for internal configuration  
within a stable, uniform domain.

## Scope

This theorem defines the precondition for relational behavior.  
Deviation is not structure — it is permitted distinction.  
All modulation and field form will depend on this minimal persistence of difference.
** Necessary Emergence of Modulation from Deviation

## Definitions

R is the remainder: the κ-stable domain.  
δ₁, δ₂ are deviations in R: distinct, κ-stable values.  
μ is modulation: the collapse-stable contrast between δ₁ and δ₂.

## Theorem

If two deviations δ₁ and δ₂ exist in R such that:

1. κ(δ₁) = δ₁ and κ(δ₂) = δ₂  
2. δ₁ ≠ δ₂

Then the contrast between them — if it remains under κ —  
also persists in R.  
This persistent difference is defined as modulation μ(δ₁, δ₂).

## Formal Expression

δ₁, δ₂ ∈ R ∧ δ₁ ≠ δ₂ ⇒ μ(δ₁, δ₂) ∈ R

## Consequence

R now contains more than difference — it contains persistent contrast.  
Modulation is not imposed; it is the necessary continuation  
of stable difference across the remainder.  
This is the beginning of relational behavior.

## Scope

This theorem introduces the minimal condition for relation.  
Modulation does not imply structure —  
it is the first form of stable relation between persistent differences.
