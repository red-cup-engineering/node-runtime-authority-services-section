#+TITLE: On Existing
#+DATE: [2025-10-23 Thu]

* Preface
This document presents a constructive foundation for the Relational ∞-Topos Modality (RITM).  
Each section introduces the minimal operator required for the next, following the principle:

> To claim that something exists is not trivial.  
> Existence entails distinction, recurrence, and stabilization.

Accordingly, the sequence begins in elementary set theory and proceeds, step by step,
through category theory to modal and cohesive ∞-topoi.

---

* Phase I — Existential Differentiation
Purpose: to show that “difference” and “residual” are necessary for anything to exist.

** Definition 1.1 (Inclusion Operator, Inc)
Let \(E\) be a set and \(A\subseteq E\).  
Define the *inclusion* \(\mathrm{Inc}(A,E) := i:A\hookrightarrow E\).
It represents the minimal act of distinguishing what is “in” from what is not.

** Definition 1.2 (Quotient Operator, Quo)
Define an equivalence relation on \(E\) that identifies all elements of \(A\):
\(x\sim_A y \iff (x=y)\lor(x,y\in A)\).
The canonical projection \(q:E\to E/{\sim_A}\) is denoted \(\mathrm{Quo}(i)\).

** Lemma 1.3 (Residual Inclusion, Res)
The image of \(A\) under \(q\) defines a new inclusion
\(\iota_i:q(A)\hookrightarrow E/{\sim_A}\).  
This is the *residual inclusion* \(\mathrm{Res}(i)\).

** Proposition 1.4 (Iterative Differentiation, Itr)
Define the composition
\[
\mathrm{Itr}(i) := \mathrm{Res}(\mathrm{Quo}(\mathrm{Inc}(A,E))).
\]
Each inclusion induces a residual inclusion within its quotient.
Repeated three times, the process returns to an isomorphic inclusion.

** Theorem 1.5 (Existential Recurrence)
For any inclusion \(i:A\hookrightarrow E\),
\[
(\mathrm{Itr})^3(i) \cong i.
\]
Hence existence is cyclic under differentiation: inclusion, exclusion, re-inclusion.
This establishes the first necessary operator family:  
\(\{\mathrm{Inc},\mathrm{Quo},\mathrm{Res},\mathrm{Itr}\}\).

* Phase II — Relational Categorification
Purpose: to organize distinctions into a system of morphisms.

** Definition 2.1 (Category of Inclusions, Cat)
Let \(\mathcal{C}_E\) be the category whose:
- objects are inclusions \(i:A\hookrightarrow E\);
- morphisms are commutative squares between inclusions.
\(\mathcal{C}_E\) expresses relations between distinctions.

** Lemma 2.2 (Finite Limits)
Pullbacks in \(\mathcal{C}_E\) are intersections \(A\times_E B=A\cap B\).  
Hence \(\mathcal{C}_E\) admits finite limits.

** Definition 2.3 (Iterative Functor, Fun)
Extend the residual iterator from Phase I to a functor:
\[
\mathrm{Fun}_E:\mathcal{C}_E\to\mathcal{C}_E,\quad
\mathrm{Fun}_E(i)=\mathrm{Itr}(i).
\]
** Proposition 2.4 (Functorial Recurrence)
\((\mathrm{Fun}_E)^3\simeq\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{C}_E}\).  
Existential recurrence becomes natural.

** Definition 2.5 (Terminal Inclusion, One)
Let \(\mathrm{One}:=(1\hookrightarrow1)\) be the terminal object of \(\mathcal{C}_E\).  
Its existence ensures identity and composition.

** Theorem 2.6 (Formation of Relational Category)
The operators of Phase I, organized by \(\mathcal{C}_E\), form a category
closed under composition and recurrence.
This demonstrates that relational structure arises inevitably from differentiation.

* Phase III — Sustained Stability
Purpose: to show that recurrence requires internal function spaces.

** Definition 3.1 (Slices and Pullback)
For \(p:X\hookrightarrow E\), define the slice category \(\mathcal{C}_E/X\)
whose morphisms preserve pullbacks over \(p\).

** Lemma 3.2 (Exponentials)
Each slice \(\mathcal{C}_E/X\) is cartesian closed: for \(A,B\in\mathcal{C}_E/X\),
there exists \([A,B]_X\) satisfying
\[
\mathrm{Hom}(Z\times_X A,B)\cong\mathrm{Hom}(Z,[A,B]_X).
\]

** Theorem 3.3 (Local Cartesian Closure)
Every \(\mathcal{C}_E/X\) is cartesian closed;
thus \(\mathcal{C}_E\) is locally cartesian closed.
This introduces the operators:
\(\{\mathrm{Pi},\mathrm{Hom}\}\)
corresponding to dependent products and internal Homs.

** Corollary 3.4 (Sustained Substitution)
Any structure preserving inclusion and recurrence now supports substitution
and internal mapping; stability under change is possible.

* Phase IV — Modal Structure
Purpose: to show that closure and co-closure induce the modal chain.

** Definition 4.1 (Reflector and Coreflector)
Let \(a\dashv i\) and \(j\dashv c\) be adjunctions defining stabilized and costabilized subcategories.
Each satisfies idempotence: \(a^2=a,\ c^2=c.\)

** Theorem 4.2 (Modal Quadruple)
Define
\[
\Pi^{→}:=a,\quad
\Box:=i\!\circ\!a,\quad
\Diamond:=j\!\circ\!c,\quad
\nabla^{←}:=c.
\]
Then
\[
\Pi^{→}\dashv\Box\dashv\Diamond\dashv\nabla^{←},
\quad
\Box\Diamond\simeq\Diamond\Box.
\]
This quadruple of adjoint idempotent endofunctors is the Relational ∞-Topos Modality.

* Phase V — Internal Dynamics
Purpose: to derive motion and persistence from the modal chain.

** Definition 5.1 (Comonad and Monad)
From the middle adjunction \(\Box\dashv\Diamond\), define
\[
C:=\Box\Diamond,\quad
M:=\Diamond\Box.
\]
Both satisfy the (co)monad laws with counit \(\varepsilon\) and unit \(\eta\).

** Lemma 5.2 (CoKleisli Category)
Objects: those of the base category;  
morphisms \(f:X→C Y\).  
Composition \(g\diamond f:=\varepsilon_{CY}\circ C g\circ f\) is associative.

** Theorem 5.3 (Stabilization ⇔ Coalgebra)
A morphism \(r:Y→C Y\) is idempotent in the coKleisli category
iff it is a \(C\)-coalgebra.  
Thus dynamics stabilize exactly at topological fixed points.

* Phase VI — Geometric Reflection
Purpose: to exhibit the modal structure as cohesive geometry.

** Definition 6.1 (Cohesive Model)
On the site of smooth spaces \( \mathrm{CartSp} \), define
\[
\Box(X)=\mathrm{Map}(I_d,X),\quad
\Diamond(X)=\mathrm{Map}(X,I_d),
\quad
\Pi^{→}(X)=\mathrm{colim}_{I_d\to X}\!\Hom(I_d,X),
\quad
\nabla^{←}(X)=\mathrm{lim}_{X\to I_d}\!\Hom(X,I_d).
\]
** Theorem 6.2 (Cohesion)
These functors satisfy the adjunction chain and idempotence properties,
realizing the RITM quadruple geometrically.
Idempotence follows from \( \mathrm{Map}(I_d\times I_d,X)\simeq\mathrm{Map}(I_d,X)\).

* Phase VII — Dualities and Self-Reflection
Purpose: to show the equivalence of internal dynamics and topology.

** Theorem 7.1 (Monad–Comonad Duality)
For \(C=\Box\Diamond\) and \(M=\Diamond\Box\),
there exists a natural distributive law \( \lambda:CM\simeq MC \).

** Corollary 7.2 (Particle–Field Equivalence)
Coalgebras of \(C\) and algebras of \(M\) are dual:
\[
\mathsf{Part}:\mathcal{E}^C \leftrightarrows \mathcal{E}_M:\mathsf{Field}.
\]

** Theorem 7.3 (Self-Witnessing Topos)
The presheaf category
\[
\mathsf{RITM}_* := \mathrm{Shv}(\mathrm{CoKl}(C)^{\mathrm{op}},\mathsf{Spaces})
\]
inherits the same quadruple of modalities, reproducing RITM internally.
Hence RITM constructs the topos in which it is realized.
